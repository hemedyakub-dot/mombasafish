Section two

Sea creatures

Ten animals and habitats of the Kenyan coast. Some you can eat, some you must never touch, and the difference matters.

Dolphin Pomboo · say it: pom-BOH-oh

Tursiops aduncus · Indo-Pacific bottlenose

A dolphin is not a fish. It is a mammal, like you — it breathes air, is born alive, and drinks its mother's milk.

Three true things

  • Dolphins find food using sound. They click, listen to the echo, and build a picture of what is ahead — even in dark water.
  • Each dolphin has its own whistle, a bit like a name, and others use it to call that individual.
  • Only half a dolphin's brain sleeps at a time. The other half keeps it swimming and breathing.

Where you find it here: Pods live around Wasini Island and the Kisite–Mpunguti park, close to Shimoni.

Looking after it: Protected in Kenya. We never catch or sell dolphins, and nor does any responsible fisherman.

Sea Turtle Kasa · say it: KAH-sah

Chelonia mydas · green turtle

A reptile that spends almost its whole life at sea, and comes ashore only to lay eggs in the sand.

Three true things

  • A female returns to lay her eggs on the same beach where she herself hatched, sometimes decades later.
  • The temperature of the sand decides whether the hatchlings are male or female — warmer sand makes more females.
  • Green turtles graze seagrass, which keeps the meadow growing like a mown lawn.

Where you find it here: Green and hawksbill turtles nest on Kenyan beaches, including the south coast near Shimoni.

Looking after it: All sea turtles are protected in Kenya. Catching, selling or eating them is illegal. If you find a nest, tell a ranger or a Beach Management Unit.

Coral Reef Matumbawe · say it: mah-toom-BAH-weh

Scleractinia · stony corals

Coral looks like a rock and is actually thousands of tiny animals living together, each one a bit like a miniature sea anemone.

Three true things

  • Corals have algae living inside them. The algae make food from sunlight and share it — which is why coral needs clear, shallow water.
  • When water gets too hot the coral pushes the algae out and turns white. That is called bleaching, and the coral is starving, not dead — yet.
  • A reef the size of a classroom can shelter more kinds of animal than a whole field on land.

Where you find it here: Fringing reefs run along most of the Kenyan coast, with protected reef inside Kisite–Mpunguti.

Looking after it: Never stand on coral, and never take a piece home. It grows about as fast as your fingernails — a piece the size of your hand took years.

Crab Kaa · say it: KAH-ah

Scylla serrata · mangrove mud crab

A crab wears its skeleton on the outside. To grow, it must climb out of its old shell entirely and grow a new one.

Three true things

  • A crab that has just moulted is completely soft and hides until the new shell hardens.
  • Crabs can regrow a lost claw over several moults.
  • Mud crabs live among mangrove roots and are strong enough to crack a shell with one claw.

Where you find it here: Mangrove creeks along the Kwale coast, including around Shimoni and Vanga.

Looking after it: Small crabs and females carrying eggs should always go back. That is how there are crabs next year.

Spiny Lobster Kamba Mkubwa · say it: KAHM-bah m-KOO-bwah

Panulirus spp.

An Indian Ocean lobster has no big front claws — it defends itself with long spiny antennae and a powerful tail flick.

Three true things

  • Lobsters sometimes walk across the seabed in long single-file queues, each touching the one in front.
  • They can shoot backwards fast by snapping the tail underneath them.
  • A lobster tastes and smells with its legs and antennae.

Where you find it here: Reef holes and ledges around Shimoni and Wasini.

Looking after it: Kenya sets a minimum size and closed seasons for lobster. Undersized lobsters must go back.

Seahorse Farasi wa Bahari · say it: fah-RAH-see wah bah-HAH-ree

Hippocampus kuda

A seahorse is a fish, even though it does not look like one. It swims upright and very slowly.

Three true things

  • It is the father who becomes pregnant. The female places her eggs in his pouch and he carries them until they hatch.
  • A seahorse has no stomach, so it must eat almost constantly.
  • Each eye moves separately, so it can watch for food and for danger at the same time.

Where you find it here: Seagrass beds and sheltered shallow water along the coast.

Looking after it: Seahorses are traded internationally and are protected under CITES rules. They are never something to collect.

Ray Taa · say it: TAH-ah

Himantura, Mobula and others

A ray is a flat relative of the shark, with a skeleton of cartilage rather than bone.

Three true things

  • Rays 'fly' through water by flapping their wide fins like wings.
  • Many rest buried in sand with only their eyes showing.
  • Manta rays are the biggest rays and eat nothing but plankton, filtering it as they swim.

Where you find it here: Sandy flats, seagrass and open water along the coast.

Looking after it: Manta and devil rays are protected. A stingray's barb is for defence, not attack — shuffle your feet in shallow sand and they simply move away.

Starfish Nyota ya Bahari · say it: n-YOH-tah yah bah-HAH-ree

Asteroidea

A sea star is not a fish at all. It has no brain and no blood, and moves on hundreds of tiny tube feet.

Three true things

  • Many sea stars can regrow a whole arm, and a few can regrow an entire body from one arm.
  • Some push their stomach out through their mouth to digest food outside their body.
  • They 'see' with simple light-sensing eyespots on the tip of each arm.

Where you find it here: Reef flats, rock pools and seagrass.

Looking after it: Taking a sea star out of water to photograph it can kill it. Look, then leave it where it was.

Mangrove Forest Mikoko · say it: mee-KOH-koh

Rhizophora mucronata and others

Trees that grow with their roots in salt water, standing on arching roots like stilts.

Three true things

  • Mangroves filter salt out of seawater — some push it out through their leaves, which taste salty.
  • Baby fish and prawns shelter among the roots where big predators cannot follow.
  • Mangrove forests store more carbon per hectare than most forests on land.

Where you find it here: Creeks and sheltered shores along the Kwale coast.

Looking after it: Mangroves are cut for timber and charcoal. Beekeeping in mangrove forest gives people a reason to keep the trees standing — that is where our mangrove honey comes from.

Seagrass Meadow Nyasi za Bahari · say it: n-YAH-see zah bah-HAH-ree

Thalassia, Cymodocea and others

Not seaweed — real flowering plants, with roots and seeds, growing underwater in meadows.

Three true things

  • Seagrass makes oxygen, exactly like grass on land.
  • It is the main food of green turtles and dugongs.
  • Its roots hold the sand down, which stops beaches washing away in storms.

Where you find it here: Shallow sandy areas between the beach and the reef, all along this coast.

Looking after it: Seagrass is damaged by anchors and by boats running aground. It is one of the least noticed and most valuable habitats we have.
