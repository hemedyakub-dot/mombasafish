Mchuzi wa Pweza

Octopus in Coconut Curry

Octopus rewards patience and punishes impatience. Simmer it slowly until a knife slides in without resistance, then let it finish in coconut milk. Rushed octopus is rubber; this is not.

Prep15 min
Cook75 min
Serves4
CuisineSwahili

Ingredients

  • 1 kg octopus, cleaned
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 2 onions, sliced thin
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 thumb ginger, grated
  • 1 tbsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 green chillies, split
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • A handful of fresh coriander
  • Salt to taste
  • Oil

Method

  1. Put the octopus in a heavy pot with no water — it releases plenty of its own. Cover and cook on a low heat for 45–60 minutes, until a knife pushes into the thickest part with no resistance.
  2. Lift it out, keep the liquid, and cut into bite-sized pieces.
  3. In another pan, fry the onions slowly in oil until soft and golden — eight minutes at least. This is where the sweetness comes from.
  4. Add garlic, ginger and chilli. Fry two minutes. Add the curry powder and coriander and stir for thirty seconds.
  5. Add the tomatoes and cook down until the oil separates out at the edges.
  6. Return the octopus with a little of its cooking liquid. Pour in the coconut milk. Simmer gently 15 minutes — do not boil hard, or the coconut splits.
  7. Finish with lime juice and fresh coriander. Serve with rice or chapati.

Tips from the counter

  • Freezing octopus before cooking genuinely tenderises it — ice crystals break down the muscle fibres. A frozen-then-thawed octopus cooks softer than a fresh one.
  • The rule is fast or slow. Under 10 minutes or over 45. Anything in between is where rubber lives.
  • Some coastal cooks drop a copper coin or a cork in the pot. It does nothing, but the tradition is charming.
