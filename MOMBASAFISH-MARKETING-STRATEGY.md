# MombasaFish — Marketing Strategy
**Date:** 6 August 2026
**Constraints this plan is built around:** zero budget · capacity-constrained · B2C first, B2B to follow

---

## 1. The diagnosis

You told me three things. Read together they point somewhere specific, and it is not where most marketing advice goes.

- **Zero budget** — no paid acquisition
- **Capacity is roughly at its limit** — you can handle what you handle now, maybe a little more
- **You want households first, restaurants later**

The second one governs everything.

**You do not have a demand problem. You have a capacity problem wearing a marketing problem's clothes.**

Standard advice at this stage is "get more customers." For you that advice is actively dangerous. Your business rests entirely on one promise — the fish arrives fresh, on time, as described. Take one order more than you can properly handle and you break that promise. In a fresh-food business a broken promise does not cost you one customer; it costs you that customer, everyone they tell, and the reputation the whole thing stands on. On a coast where word travels fast, that is not recoverable in a season.

So the strategy is not *more orders*.

> **The strategy is: more value from every order you were going to take anyway, and a deliberate plan to raise capacity before you raise demand.**

Everything below serves that sentence.

---

## 2. What NOT to do

Naming these first, because the temptation will come.

**❌ Do not run ads.** You have no budget, and even if you found some, paying to generate demand you cannot fulfil is paying to damage yourself.

**❌ Do not chase follower counts.** Ten thousand followers who never buy cost you time you do not have. Your Instagram following is not an asset; your WhatsApp list is.

**❌ Do not discount to drive volume.** Discounting increases orders and reduces margin — exactly the wrong direction when orders are the constrained resource and margin is the free one.

**❌ Do not launch a loyalty points scheme.** Complexity you would have to administer by hand for a benefit that arrives in a year.

**❌ Do not expand to new counties yet.** Eight is already more than you can serve deeply. Depth beats breadth when capacity is the limit.

**❌ Do not post daily on five platforms.** You are one person. Pick two, do them properly, ignore the rest.

## 3. The five plays, in order

Ranked by return on the only currency you have: your time.

---

### PLAY 1 — Raise the average order, not the order count
*Cost: nothing · Effort: low · Impact: immediate*

This is the whole strategy in one move. If you take the same number of orders next month but each is worth 30% more, you have grown 30% without touching capacity.

**What to do:**

**Lead with bundles, not kilos.** You already have the ladder — 1,500 / 2,900 / 3,600 / 5,700 / 9,500. When someone messages "how much is changu," do not answer with a price per kilo. Answer with the price, then: *"Most families your size take the Familia Box — 4 kg for 2,900, works out cheaper than buying them separately."* You have just moved a 700-shilling order to 2,900.

**Attach one thing to every order.** The cheapest sale you will ever make is the second item to someone already buying. Three natural attachments:
- Mangrove honey (900) — light, no cold chain, high margin, an easy yes
- Dried prawns (2,000) — shelf-stable, no extra handling
- A bump from cocktail prawns to tiger prawns — same handling, +1,400/kg

**Shift the mix upward.** A kilo of lobster and a kilo of tafi take almost identical effort to buy, clean, pack and ship. One earns 3,300 and the other 700. When capacity is your constraint, **every kilo of capacity should carry as much value as it can.** This does not mean stop selling tafi — it means make sure people know the lobster, the prawns and the boxes exist.

**Set a minimum for upcountry.** Sending a 2 kg order to Nakuru consumes as much of your day as a 10 kg one. A gentle minimum outside Mombasa — say 4 kg — protects your capacity without turning anyone away rudely.

> **Target: move the average order from wherever it is now to 1.5× that, within 90 days.** Measure it. It is the single number that matters most this quarter.

### PLAY 2 — Estate group buying
*Cost: nothing · Effort: medium · Impact: high — this is the standout play*

This is the one idea in this document I would push hardest, because it solves acquisition and capacity **at the same time**, which almost nothing does.

**How it works.** One delivery to one apartment block or gated estate, split between eight or ten households who ordered together. You do one trip, one cooler run, one point of contact. They get fresher fish and share the transport cost so each pays a fraction of what solo delivery costs.

**Why it is so strong for you specifically:**
- **It reduces your cost per order rather than raising it** — the opposite of normal growth
- **Every Kenyan estate already has a WhatsApp group** — the distribution already exists and you do not have to build it
- **It is referral marketing that does not feel like marketing** — the neighbour who organises it is vouching for you
- **It concentrates your deliveries** — ten orders, one destination
- **It scales without ads** — one satisfied estate leads to the next through the same social networks

**How to start, concretely:**
1. Look through your existing orders for any two customers in the same estate or neighbourhood. Start there.
2. Message that customer: *"If you can get five neighbours together, I'll deliver to the estate gate and split the transport between you. Everyone pays less and the fish arrives colder."*
3. Give the organiser something real — a free kilo, or their transport waived. Their effort deserves payment.
4. Set an order-day rhythm: *"Estate deliveries to Kileleshwa go out every Thursday. Orders close Wednesday 4pm."* A fixed day lets you plan capacity instead of reacting to it.
5. When it works once, ask the organiser to introduce you to one other estate.

**Target: two estates running on a fixed weekly rhythm within 60 days.**

### PLAY 3 — Make the second order automatic
*Cost: nothing · Effort: low · Impact: compounding*

A repeat customer costs nothing to acquire, already trusts you, needs no persuading, and is far more likely to buy a bundle than a first-timer. Your database says 30 orders. The most valuable question you can ask is: **how many of those were the same person twice?**

If the answer is "hardly any", that is your single biggest and cheapest opportunity.

**What to do:**

**Follow up every order, once, three days later.** One message: *"Hope the changu was good — how did you cook it?"* Not a sales message. It does three things at once: it catches problems before they become bad word of mouth, it gets you a testimonial in the customer's own words, and it keeps you present without nagging.

**Build a broadcast list, not a group.** WhatsApp broadcast lists send to everyone individually — no group noise, no strangers seeing each other's numbers. Two messages a week maximum: what landed today, and one recipe or piece of ocean content. People must have your number saved for broadcasts to arrive, so ask them to save it on the first order.

**Use "today's catch" as your recurring hook.** It is honest scarcity — genuinely limited, genuinely perishable — and it never gets stale because it changes daily. *"Boats came in with sailfish this morning. Eight kilos, that's all."* That is a real message, and it is more compelling than any discount.

**Ask directly for the referral.** After a good delivery: *"If you know someone who'd like this, send them my number."* Most people are happy to and simply never think of it.

**Target: 40% of orders coming from repeat customers within 90 days.**

### PLAY 4 — Two channels, done properly
*Cost: nothing · Effort: medium, sustained*

You are one person. Two channels, chosen for what they actually do:

**Google Business Profile — do this first, it takes one evening.**
Free, and it is what appears when someone in Mombasa searches *"fish near me"* or *"seafood delivery Mombasa."* That searcher has the highest purchase intent of anyone you will meet. Fill it completely: every category, opening hours, twenty photos of real fish, the products, and the link to your site. Then ask five happy customers to leave a Google review. Five real reviews will do more for local search than a month of Instagram.

**TikTok — one platform, done seriously.**
TikTok reach in Kenya is unusually strong and it is entirely free. Fish content performs well because the process is inherently watchable. You have access nobody else has.

Post three times a week, 20–40 seconds, no editing, no music, phone held steady:
- A boat landing at Shimoni
- One clean fillet cut, start to finish
- An octopus changing colour on the slab
- Ice going into a cooler
- Someone eating something you delivered
- *"Which fish has no bones?"* — answer in fifteen seconds

**The rule that matters: film work, not adverts.** People scroll past adverts and stop for work. Your job is not to be interesting; it is to point the camera at something that already is.

**Instagram and Facebook: repost the TikToks, nothing more.** No original effort.

### PLAY 5 — Let the website do the work you already paid for
*Cost: nothing · Effort: minimal · Impact: builds over months*

You have 73 pages carrying real information. That asset earns nothing until people arrive at it, and it will start earning in October, not this week.

**In the meantime, use it manually:**
- Never describe a fish over WhatsApp again. Send `mombasafish.com/fish/nguru`. It answers every question, shows the price, and looks like a real business.
- When someone asks how to cook something, send the recipe page.
- When someone asks whether it is really fresh, send `/shimoni`.
- Approach one school with Ocean Explorers. It costs nothing and it is the kind of thing local press writes about.

**Do the two Search Console jobs** — sitemap resubmitted, indexing requested on your best pages. Then leave it alone for a month.

## 4. The capacity question you have to answer

Everything above is a holding pattern until you answer this: **what exactly is the limit?**

It is one of four things, and the fix is different for each:

| If the limit is… | Then the fix is… |
|---|---|
| **Cash to buy the catch** | Faster payment, higher-margin mix, and prepayment on bundles. This is the most common limit and the least discussed. |
| **Your own time** | One trusted person for packing and dispatch, even part-time on delivery days |
| **Cold storage** | The single capital purchase worth saving for |
| **Transport** | A fixed weekly delivery rhythm per county rather than reacting to each order |

**Work out which one it is this month.** Until you know, you are optimising in the dark — and every growth decision after this depends on the answer.

## 5. When to switch to B2B

You chose households first. That is reasonable and I would not argue with it. But one observation worth holding onto:

**For a capacity-constrained business, B2B is more efficient per kilo, not less.** One restaurant taking 20 kg every Tuesday is one purchase decision, one delivery, one relationship, one payment — against twenty household orders for the same volume, each with its own conversation, packing, delivery and follow-up.

So the trigger to move is not a date. It is this:

> **When your average order stops rising and your repeat rate has plateaued, B2B is your next source of growth — and it will strain your capacity less than the same volume in household orders.**

Realistically that is three to six months away. When it comes, `/wholesale` is already built and waiting. The approach then is simple: three restaurants, personally, with fish in hand. Not email.

## 6. What to measure

Four numbers. Write them on paper at the end of each month — you do not need software.

| Number | Why it matters | Target by November |
|---|---|---|
| **Average order value** | The main growth lever while capacity is fixed | 1.5× today |
| **% of orders from repeat customers** | The cheapest revenue there is | 40% |
| **Orders per week** | Watch it does not exceed what you can serve well | Flat, deliberately |
| **Google reviews** | The trust asset that compounds and costs nothing | 15 |

Ignore followers, likes, and website visitors for now. None of them are money.
